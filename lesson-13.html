About 10–15 minutes with the task · Read, attempt, then explain

The idea

A check can be answered by moving the king, capturing the checking piece, or interposing a piece when the attack travels along a line. Each candidate must leave the king safe from every attacker. Knight checks and adjacent-piece checks cannot be blocked. In double check, the king must move because answering only one attacking line is insufficient.

Worked explanation

A bishop checking along a long diagonal may permit a block. A knight checking from f7 cannot be answered by putting a piece between knight and king.

Try it yourself

In one sliding-piece check, list the three categories before calculating. In a knight check, cross out interposition.

Reveal the task answer and reasoning

The categories help generate possibilities; the board may leave only one legal move or none. No legal evasion while in check means checkmate.

The mistake to watch for

Finding a block that stops one line but exposes another.

Close the lesson and test your recall

Can you block a knight check? No. Move the king or legally capture the checking knight.
